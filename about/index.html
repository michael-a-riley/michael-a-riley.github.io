About

A workforce systems specialist who reads land the same way he reads skills.

I'm Michael Riley. For about fifteen years I've worked on how people acquire skills, how that learning gets recognised — or doesn't — and how training systems connect to real livelihoods. The thread running through all of it is the same: the most important knowledge in a system is usually the part no one has written down.

The systems work

My core practice is technical and vocational education and training (TVET), recognition of prior learning, and rural livelihoods, mostly across the Middle East and North Africa. I've worked with the World Bank on education and TVET in Yemen, Saudi Arabia, and Djibouti, run livelihoods fieldwork in India, managed a training institution, and handled sub-grants administration at the Population Council. More recently I've been in contact with GIZ's STEP programme on skills and employment in Syria.

The research

I hold an MSc from the Department of Landscape Design and Ecosystem Management at the American University of Beirut, an MA from Johns Hopkins SAIS, and a BA from the University of Chicago. My master's thesis — defended in 2026 — studied how displaced Syrian agricultural workers in Lebanon's Bekaa Valley read environmental conditions and sustain farming systems through skill that the formal sector never records. That work is being prepared as a journal article and a conference paper. You can read the article draft here.

The farm design

The thesis pushed me from studying land to designing for it. I now make site-specific permaculture master plans: I read a piece of land's sun, slope, water and soil, then lay out where everything goes — growing zones, a water system that catches rain high and moves it downhill by gravity, a planting palette suited to the climate, and a phased roadmap of what to build and plant in what order. I'm offering first designs free right now, to build real cases on the ground. See an example plan.

Where I am

I'm based in Damascus. My work and my commitments point toward Syria's reconstruction — its skills systems, its land, and the people rebuilding both. I work in English (native), Arabic (Levantine professional working proficiency, able to train and facilitate), and French, with a handful of other languages at lower levels.
