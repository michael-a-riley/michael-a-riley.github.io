About

I'm Michael Riley. For about fifteen years I've worked on technical training, recognition of prior learning, and rural livelihoods — how people pick up skills, whether those skills get formally recognised, and how training connects to actual work. A lot of what I do comes down to the same problem: the knowledge that keeps a system running is usually the part nobody bothered to write down.

The systems work

My core practice is technical and vocational education and training (TVET), recognition of prior learning, and rural livelihoods, mostly across the Middle East and North Africa. I've worked with the World Bank on education and TVET in Yemen, Saudi Arabia, and Djibouti, run livelihoods fieldwork in India, managed a training institution, and handled sub-grants administration at the Population Council. More recently I've been in contact with GIZ's STEP programme on skills and employment in Syria.

The research

I hold an MSc from the Department of Landscape Design and Ecosystem Management at the American University of Beirut, an MA from Johns Hopkins SAIS, and a BA from the University of Chicago. My master's thesis — defended in 2026 — studied how displaced Syrian agricultural workers in Lebanon's Bekaa Valley read environmental conditions and sustain farming systems through skill that the formal sector never records. That work is being prepared as a journal article and a conference paper. You can read the article draft here.

The farm design

The thesis got me into design work. I now make site-specific permaculture plans: I read a piece of land's sun, slope, water and soil, then lay out where everything goes — growing zones, a water system that catches rain high and moves it downhill by gravity, planting suited to the climate, and a five-year roadmap of what to build and plant in what order. I'm doing first designs free right now to build real cases. See an example plan.

Where I am

I'm based in Damascus. My work points toward Syria — its skills systems, its land, and the rebuilding underway. I work in English (native), Arabic (Levantine professional working proficiency, able to train and facilitate), and French, plus a few other languages at lower levels.
