Farm Design

A real plan for a real piece of land — not generic advice.

I design farms: site-specific permaculture master plans built around one particular piece of land. The output is a plan someone could hold in their hands and start on.

What a design is

I read the land first — sun, slope, where water runs and where it's lost, what's already growing, where the wind blows. Then I lay it out:

  • Zones, from the doorstep out to the wild edge, organised by how often each part needs you.
  • A water system of ponds and swales that catches rain high in the landscape and walks it downhill by gravity — no pumps.
  • A planting palette of trees, herbs, vines and ground cover chosen for that specific climate and soil.
  • Animals placed for the work they do, not just what they give.
  • A phased roadmap — usually five years — of what to build and plant, in what order.

Why water comes first

Water is the part that matters most. The aim is to slow heavy rain and sink it into the ground instead of letting it run off and strip the soil. Held in the land, the same rain that floods you is there in the dry months too.
